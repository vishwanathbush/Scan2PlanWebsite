The Castle

We chose the Paine Castle in Troy, New York, as the case study for our standards because of its custom geometry. The high level of detail that historic preservation needs is one of the things we do best, and the same house shows how custom geometry reads in generic form in the LoD 200 model. As the model moves from generic to specific, toward the point where it is identical to the real thing, level of development is a language for detail.

We modeled the house three times, at level of development 200, 300 and 350, so an architect can see what each level holds before writing it into a scope.

As Seen On TV

The Paine Castle appears in Martin Scorsese's The Age of Innocence and in HBO's The Gilded Age. When it was finished in 1896 it was called the Grandest House in Troy, then one of the wealthiest cities in the United States. The alumni of the Alpha Tau chapter of Pi Kappa Phi at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute have owned it for over 70 years. Their foundation plans a complete renovation: restore the house, and bring new adaptive uses into it.

The model arrives. Revit file, point cloud, maybe a 3D Tour. The proposal promised “high accuracy” and “a detailed model,” which has mostly meant “we tried our best.”

Six months later a framer pulls a dimension off that model and misses the wall by two inches. Rework eats 9% of a project by the conservative count and 21% by the honest one. On a $1.5M renovation, that is $135,000 to $315,000. Half of it traces to bad project data. And in a 2020 study of 517 renovation change orders, the most expensive kind was the one caused by an existing condition nobody documented. Nobody pins it on the model. By then the file is three phases old. No one connects a crooked wall to a drawing signed off last spring.

  • Steel fabricators ship certified mill test reports with every beam.
  • Concrete batch plants ship cylinder break reports with every pour.
  • Elevator inspectors issue operating certificates before the cab carries a passenger.
  • Scan-to-BIM vendors ship a Revit file and a handshake.

The Certificate of Assurance is that report. It ships with every S2P project: a one-page verification record of twelve criteria. Each is stated against a published standard: BIMForum for model detail, USIBD for accuracy, BOMA for gross area.

S2P stands behind it with three written guarantees.

  1. Transparency: our 12-point assurance standard is defined in writing before work begins and evidenced on the Certificate at delivery.
  2. Price-Match: if a competitor proposes the same scope for less, S2P matches the price, provided the competitor defines their own standards or accepts S2P’s.
  3. Lifetime Support: unlimited revisions within scope, and every file kept indefinitely at no charge. All three are defined in full in S2P’s Client Guide.

Here are the twelve points in the standard. Each one is a line you can write into your next scope of work and hold any vendor to.

12-Point Assurance Standard§1

7 · Before mobilization

  1. Deliverables. The exact files and formats you receive, named — Revit version, point cloud format, any derived outputs. No “and supporting files.”
  2. LoD. (Level of Development): how much of each element is actually modeled, stated separately for architecture, structure, and MEPF. see BIMForum LoD Specification (2025).
  3. Cloud LoA. (Level of Accuracy): This is measured accuracy, how far the point cloud may sit from the real building, as a number with a tolerance, on the USIBD LoA Specification (v3.1). LoA 40 means under an eighth of an inch.
  4. Model LoA. This is represented accuracy, how closely the model represents that cloud. A model can be a generic or specific representation of an accurate cloud. Two accuracies are stated apart. LoA 30 is under a half-inch.
  5. Coordinates. The shared or approximated coordinate system the model is built in, named before the scan.
  6. Datum. The vertical reference the heights are measured from, named.
  7. Control. How the scan ties to known survey control points, how the scan passes accuracy gates.

3 · During production

  1. Registration. Registration is the step that joins separate scans into one dataset. The software reports an average error for that join. The Certificate carries that number, inside a stated tolerance. When that number strays, the geometry looks accurate, measurements that agree with each other, pass every check on screen, but disagree with the building. By the time anyone finds out, it’s a change order.
  1. Coverage. What the scan could not reach, named on the record — a locked room, a space nobody opened — so the design team plans honestly around the gap.
  2. Assumptions. Every place the modeler inferred instead of measured - gaps in scanning, visibility, logged. Otherwise they surface later as RFIs. The avoidable kind.

2 · After delivery

  1. Acceptance. What “correct” means, written as a number and agreed before the model ships. Most as-builts run on the alternative. That’s not a standard. That’s a mood.
  2. Support. Corrections within the documented LoD and LoA, file and coordinate help, unlimited revisions within scope. S2P keeps every project file — Certificate, point cloud, model, assumption log — with no expiration and no storage fee. Phase 2 lands three years later and the building is already measured: your architect opens a verified and supported model instead of scoping a new scan.

Why It Took This Long§3

The scanners have been accurate for a decade, so the holdup was never the technology. Scan-to-BIM grew out of equipment sales: surveying firms that added modeling, modeling firms that added scanning. No one’s core business was the verified model, so no one wrote it down. S2P analyzed 131 scan-to-BIM firms: fewer than 3% state an accuracy or detail standard, and fewer than 1% offer any guarantee.

The Risk Doesn’t Expire§4

If the existing conditions were never verified, the liability for a bad dimension does not clear when the model lands on your server. It transfers — to the firm that built on a model no one verified. A vendor who documents against these twelve has shown their work and where it was checked. A vendor who can’t may still be good; plenty are. But good without documentation is a relationship, not a professional record, and relationships don’t survive the PM leaving, the partner retiring or the project going sideways in construction.

01 · The Certificate of Assurance

One page that proves the model before you build.

Every Scan2Plan project ships with a Certificate of Assurance. It is one page that states 12 things in writing about your data and your BIM (Building Information Model). Each is checked against a published standard and verified by our Studio Manager.

How many points does your vendor state in writing? Specify the rest in your next scope of work.

The 12-point Assurance Standard
01Deliverables listedOPEN +

Every file you receive is listed by name and format: the Point Cloud (the scan itself, millions of measured points), the BIM model, 360 Photos and CAD drawings. Check delivery against the list and save it to track the project.

02LoD stated per disciplineOPEN +

LoD is the Level of Detail and Development, how much of the building the model represents. The certificate states the LoD for each discipline in project scope. Our 4 disciplines are: Architecture, Structure, MEPF (mechanical, electrical, plumbing & fire safety) and Landscape.

03LoA stated for point cloudOPEN +

LoA stands for Level of Accuracy, a scale published by the U.S. Institute of Building Documentation (USIBD). The certificate states it for measured accuracy, which is the point cloud tolerance to the real building. Our typical tolerance is LoA 40 (under 1/8 inch).

04LoA stated for modelOPEN +

The certificate also states the Level of Accuracy for represented accuracy, which is the model set against the point cloud. Our typical average tolerance is LoA 30 (under 1/2 inch), held as a guideline. A model draws walls flat and plumb and real walls are neither, so a model has looser accuracy than a point cloud.

05Coordinate basis statedOPEN +

Every model sits in a coordinate system: an origin and a set of axes that give each point its location. The certificate states which kind the project uses: arbitrary, approximate (oriented on the map by address), survey-grade (georeferenced to real-world coordinates), or shared from another project’s coordinates. Approximate is our default.

06Level datum statedOPEN +

The level datum is the height that the model calls 0'-0". We agree on it before modeling, so that every team on the project coordinates from the same elevations.

07Control documentedOPEN +

On most projects we scan from a tripod (terrestrial LiDAR). The overlapping scans check each other, which USIBD calls B-Validation. On larger projects (over 100K sqft) we can scan with a realtime (SLAM) scanner for speed. A second precision instrument captures a control data set. We use it to eliminate drift in the SLAM data, the errors that can build up in a moving scanner. USIBD calls this C-Validation. The certificate documents the control data and its Level of Accuracy.

08Registration metricsOPEN +

Registration joins the separate scans into a unified point cloud. The certificate reports two figures: the average percentage of scan overlap, (at least 50%), and the average disagreement between redundant points (RMS, the root mean square), measured in the field and in the studio. The crew checks its scan data before leaving. Studio registration tightens this to LoA 40 (under 1/8 inch).

09Coverage limits statedOPEN +

A scanner records only what it can see. Any room or area it could not reach is named on the certificate, with the reason. Total square footage is audited by BOMA Gross Area standards, and you are only billed for the square footage modeled.

10Assumptions loggedOPEN +

Where the modeler had to assume something that the scan did not show, the certificate lists the element and what the assumed model rests on.

11Acceptance definedOPEN +

Acceptance is the test that a delivery must pass. The scan must meet its registration figures and cover the agreed scope. The model must meet the agreed LoD and pass our 4-Stage quality control.

12Support scope definedOPEN +

The certificate says what support follows delivery: corrections within scope, your scan kept on file with no end date, upgradable models, and help coordinating with the other consultants and vendors as the project moves forward.

What we don’t promise

Concealed conditions. Small pipes and conduit, unless contracted. Structural capacity or code compliance. Stamped surveys or documents.

01.16.8.15 Approximate GeometryLOD 200 · ARCHITECTURE

6.8.16 The model shall depict the building’s architectural envelope in its essential geometric form. Exterior and interior walls use standard wall profiles at accurate thickness and height, while floors, roofs, and ceilings are placed at true elevations; roof and ceiling planes that are intentionally sloped are modeled at their measured pitch. A simple chimney opening is included where present. No decorative trims, surface sweeps, custom rail profiles, or in-situ wall/ceiling carvings are modeled.

02Structure
02.16.8.23 Approximate GeometryLOD 200 · STRUCTURE

6.8.24 The structural existing-conditions model will be delivered at LoD 200. The model will capture the building’s primary load-bearing framework in outline form. Columns, beams, trusses, and major floor or roof slabs are shown at correct locations and approximate cross-section sizes, allowing users to understand spans, bays, and general member hierarchy. Roof structural objects are represented as flat framing without intentional slopes. Braces, secondary framing, and any connection hardware are omitted. Member profiles may use standard shapes rather than custom as-built sections, and foundations are indicated only by their basic footprint or pad. This schematic-grade structural model supports conceptual studies, massing alignment, and early discipline coordination without implying fabrication-level precision. LoD 200 provides a lower-detail structural model option focused on broad structural geometry, suitable when detailed connections or precise member dimensions are not needed in the deliverable.

02.26.8.25 Accurate GeometryLOD 300 · STRUCTURE

6.8.26 The structural existing-conditions model will be delivered at LoD 300. At this level, every principal structural member—columns, beams, joists, trusses, and floor/roof framing—appears at its measured size, shape, and elevation so that clearances and quantities can be taken directly from the model. Roof structure aligns with observed slopes, including rafters or joists that follow the roof pitch, but purlins, webs, and braces remain excluded. Members end cleanly at supports with no discrete plates, bolts, or weld objects. Foundations are depicted with accurate plan size and top-of-concrete elevations. The resulting model provides a trustworthy as-built frame for clash detection with architectural or MEPF systems, while deferring the minutiae of connections and secondary bracing to later detail stages. The LoD 300 structural model alternate is effectively a precise as-built structural frame, enabling design and analysis integration while omitting the minutiae of connections which can be assumed or detailed separately if needed.

6.8.28 The structural existing-conditions model will be delivered at LoD 350, incorporating a high level of detail including connection features. This highest structural level rebuilds the framing system with full secondary members and connection detail. In addition to the accurately sized columns, beams, joists, trusses, and sloped roof framing of LoD 300, the model now includes braces, purlins, webs, knee braces, and other secondary elements wherever they appear. Critical connections are explicitly shown—beam-to-column seats, clip angles, base plates, gusset plates, anchor bolts, bearing pads, shelf angles, and similar hardware—sized to reflect their field-verified footprint. Where connections are concealed yet spatially significant (e.g., oversized end plates), they are modeled as simplified solids occupying true extents. Foundation details such as grade-beams or pier caps are included when visible or documented. With both member geometry and connection footprints present, this LoD 350 structural model allows exhaustive clash checking, fabrication planning, and sequencing, serving as a construction-ready reference for the existing structure, capturing how members join and interact.

03.16.8.31 Approximate GeometryLOD 200 · MEPF

6.8.32 The MEPF (Mechanical, Electrical, Plumbing, and Fire Protection) model of existing conditions will be provided at LoD 200. The model will include the major distribution lines and system components in a diagrammatic and approximate manner. The model captures primary building-services infrastructure in an intentionally schematic form. Main supply and return HVAC ducts are shown with generic cross-sections and slopes, including any flex connectors larger than eight inches but omitting branch take-offs or accessories. Plumbing, hydronic, and fire-protection piping appear only where nominal diameters exceed two inches, and electrical conduits over three inches are routed as single lines or extrusions; smaller lines and secondary branches are not modeled. Cable trays are depicted as horizontal default trays without inclination, and major equipment—air-handling units, chillers, boilers, main switchboards, generators, pumps—is represented by simple volumetric blocks in approximate locations. Default ceiling grilles, diffusers, exhaust fans, sprinkler heads, and wall devices (switches, receptacles, hydrants) are placed generically to flag service zones, but no valves, dampers, coils, hangers, or seismic supports are included. The result is a high-level services map that conveys spatial reservation and system presence while minimizing modeling effort. It is intended for contexts where a basic record of existing building systems is needed without the expense of detailed modeling; it is not suitable for fine clash detection or precise quantity calculations, but provides a foundation that can be further detailed in future phases if required.

03.26.8.33 Accurate GeometryLOD 300 · MEPF

6.8.34 The MEPF (Mechanical, Electrical, Plumbing, and Fire Protection) model of existing conditions will be provided at LoD 300, providing a detailed and accurate representation of major building services. Building-services geometry becomes dimensionally dependable for coordination. All principal and secondary distribution elements are modeled to their true diameters or cross-sections down to 1½ inches for piping and conduit; branch routing reflects actual slopes where present. Ductwork carries generic accessories such as elbows, tees, dampers, and simple transition pieces, and flex-duct connectors are included at main connections but not extended to every terminal. Cable trays follow their observed paths—including inclines or drops—and display correct widths and side-rail depths, though splice plates and splice-bolts are omitted. Mechanical equipment (air-handling units, rooftop units, pumps, VAV boxes, etc.) is shown with accurate overall dimensions and inlet/outlet stubs. Electrical panels, transformers, and generators are modeled to correct footprint and height. Plumbing and fire-protection systems include shut-off valves, meters, and representative sprinkler risers; pipe and duct hangers or seismic braces are still excluded. This LoD 300 model supports reliable clash detection, space validation, and preliminary quantity take-offs without the granularity of every support or specialty fitting. It delivers an accurate depiction of existing building systems suitable for most design and renovation planning activities, without going into the ultra-fine granularity of every support or minor fitting.

Ducts and pipes of all sizes are modeled with their complete suite of fittings and specialties: reducers, flex connectors, dampers, strainers, coils, expansion joints, test ports, and cleanouts. Flex-duct networks extend to every terminal, and pipe and duct hangers, trapezes, seismic braces, and cable-tray supports are represented where they occupy space. Cable trays are custom-profiled, with true side-rail geometry, all inclination changes, horizontal bends, tees, and dropouts. Conduits are shown individually—including small diameter—and include junction boxes and stub-ups at equipment or device terminations. Mechanical equipment is modeled with connection flanges, maintenance clearances, and curb or inertia-block details; electrical gear shows conduit entries and working-space envelopes. Plumbing and fire systems depict every valve, backflow preventer, drain, and sprinkler head at exact coordinates. With both geometry and support hardware present, the LoD 350 MEPF model delivers zero-tolerance coordination and enables fabrication drawings, installation sequencing, and comprehensive interference checking across all trades. This is suited for projects requiring zero-tolerance coordination (e.g., complex retrofits or dense mechanical spaces), as it delivers the highest confidence that the model contains every crucial MEPF element present in the facility.

FAQ

Why Scan2Plan
01What makes Scan2Plan different?

The detail you specify, drawn the same way on every job. One service, from the scan to the model, done for you: highly detailed BIM at scale, for adaptive reuse, historic preservation and cultural heritage. A dedicated BIM team draws each set by hand. A separate reviewer, never the person who drew it, checks it against the point cloud. Our Studio Manager verifies the scope. We test-load the files in your software before delivery. Every set comes with a Certificate of Assurance listing what was measured, assumed and left out.

02Why not measure it myself, use the old drawings, or use a Matterport or an iPhone?

Measuring buildings puts no equity into your firm. Your own projects and your design do. Old drawings and a tape measure leave gaps. A Matterport tour or an iPhone scan looks right on screen but cannot check its own measurements. You pay for it later in coordination meetings, RFIs and change orders.

Cost
03How much does it cost?

It depends on area, building type, disciplines, level of detail and location. You get a proposal with the fee within 24 hours. If another firm can match our published standards, we’ll match their price.

04Will the bill match the estimate?

We measure the gross area on site and bill only for the area we model. Your rate per square foot is locked in the proposal. If the measured area is more than the estimate, we tell you before we model and you choose: pay for the extra area, or keep to the estimate and we model less. A smaller area than estimated can cost less.

05Is there a minimum job size?

No, but our minimum pricing is 3,000 sq ft. For landscape only, 5 acres.

06Who pays for the scan: my firm or my client?

Either. Your firm can hire us as its consultant and bill the owner. Or the owner can hire us directly, and you keep your professional scope.

What you get
07What do I actually get?

A Revit or Archicad model, the point cloud, 360 photos, the Square Footage Audit and the Certificate of Assurance. CAD drawings, other BIM formats, Matterport 3D Tour, Drone Photos or Georeferencing are options.

08Will it work in my software and my template?

Yes. Models are built in Revit or Archicad, to your template and families if you send them. They export to AutoCAD, SketchUp, Rhino, Vectorworks and IFC.

09Can I get 2D CAD drawings?

Yes, as an option: plans, reflected ceiling plans, sections, elevations and roof plans. We clean them up with unbroken lines, colors, line weights and clean layers. CAD conversion costs extra and adds a week.

10How much detail do I need?

Only what the project uses. LoD is the level of detail and development. LoD 200 is approximate and schematic, 300 is accurate and construction-ready, 350 is precise and highly detailed. You can mix them by area: 350 for a historic lobby, 200 for storage rooms. Start lower if you like: we keep the scan, so a model can be raised to a higher LoD later without another site visit.

If it’s wrong
11What happens if something is wrong or missing, and who pays?

We fix any errors or omissions at no charge, generally by the next day, with no limit on rounds within scope. New areas, added disciplines, more detail and changes to the building after delivery are change orders.

12How accurate is it?

The point cloud is within 1/8 inch (3 mm). The model averages within 1/2 inch (12 mm), held as a guideline. Those are Level of Accuracy 40 and 30 in the U.S. Institute of Building Documentation standard. The model tolerance is wider because modeling software draws walls flat and plumb, and real walls lean and bow.

13Will it show what’s inside walls, above ceilings or below grade?

No. A scanner records only what it can see. We can scan above-ceiling systems wherever ceiling tiles are removed.

14What if areas are inaccessible on scan day?

We walk the building with you first, and your representative opens every space. A room we cannot reach is named on the Certificate of Assurance, with the reason. A return visit carries a charge.

Timing
15How soon can you shoot, and when do I get the model?

On site within 1 week of signing. Point cloud and 360 photos within 1 week of the scan. The model in 2–4 weeks, typical; most jobs are done in three. Complex jobs take 5–7 weeks. Rush delivery is available.

One project manager, from signing to delivery. They schedule the scan, send you the point cloud, the 360 photos and the Square Footage Audit, keep you posted, deliver the model and ensure your satisfaction.
